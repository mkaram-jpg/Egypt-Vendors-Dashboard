Executive summary

Home care carries the margin, staples carry the traffic, and working capital is the binding constraint

Key findings

  1. The market is large but still mostly traditional. Small local grocers hold roughly 60% of food retail (over US$13 bn), per the USDA FAS Retail Foods report. Supermarkets and hypermarkets are cited at about 26% of sales. Modern chains are expanding fast at the discount end: BIM had 445 stores at end-2025 and targets 1,000 by end-2026, and Kazyon reportedly has more than 1,000 stores. VERIFIED/REPORTED
  2. Commodity staples cannot fund a private-label business on their own. Packed sugar averaged about 35.68 EGP/kg on 16 Nov 2026, while Ministry of Supply initiative outlets sold it at 25 EGP. With bulk sugar estimated at about 24 EGP/kg, our modelled gross margin on a 1 kg own-brand pack is only about 4–5% after a 10% retailer margin. Rice is about 8%. ESTIMATE
  3. Home and personal care is where private-label economics work. Branded reference prices are high: Persil gel 1 L at 102.50 EGP, Pril 1 L about 60–69 EGP, Lux 115 g at 25.99 EGP. A private label priced 25–35% below the brand can still leave the retailer a 25% margin and us an estimated 16–31% gross margin. REPORTED prices · ESTIMATE costs
  4. Contract manufacturing capacity exists. Several Egyptian home-care factories publicly describe private-label or contract services, including ADCO, Scitra, Greenline and Madar. None is yet qualified by us, so each needs an RFQ, a site audit and samples.
  5. Working capital, not margin, decides feasibility. Egyptian B2B contracts typically set 30–60 day terms, but actual payment runs 60–90+ days (Allianz Trade). With the central bank's overnight lending rate at 21% (29 Oct 2026), financing receivables is expensive. In the base case the model needs a peak funding of — before it turns cash-positive.
  6. Start with Private Label as a Service (Model B) for regional chains using contract manufacturing (Model A sourcing). Retailers own the brand and carry part of the demand risk, and a setup fee funds development. Launch our own consumer brand (Opportunity A) only after 6–12 months of sell-through data.

Recommendation

Conditional GO for a 90-day validation pilot. Not yet a GO for full investment. Run a pilot with 2–3 regional chains (5–25 branches each) and 5–6 SKUs, led by home care: dishwashing liquid 1 L and 500 ml, laundry liquid 1 L, floor cleaner 1 L and liquid hand soap 500 ml. Add rice 1 kg as a price-entry staple. Supply sugar only as a fee-based service for retailers that want a price-entry SKU, never as our own-brand margin product. Before committing growth capital, hit the go/no-go criteria in Section 12.

What we know about commercial terms

  • Payment terms: contractual credit is usually 30–60 days, but actual settlement often stretches to 60–90 days or longer, and late payment is common (Allianz Trade, Egypt collection profile). REPORTED · not retailer-specific.
  • Listing fees, rebates, retailer margins: no public source was found. The model uses ASSUMPTION retailer margins of 10–12% on staples and 25% on home care, plus a 3% back-margin rebate. Validate both in buyer interviews.
  • Carrefour Egypt planned to introduce over 1,000 private-label SKUs in 2024 and states that 99% of its supply chain is locally sourced. That makes it a credible but demanding target: expect audits and food-safety standards. REPORTED company claims.
  • Private-label acceptance: an older Nielsen study reports that 73% of Egyptian respondents think store-brand quality has improved. A 2018 Nielsen deck shows the top 5 categories make up 63% of private-label sales. McKinsey (2026) finds private-label penetration below 10% in four of five MENA markets. Together these point to early-stage but rising acceptance. REPORTED

Governorate-level opportunity

Regional chains are the better first door. They typically have shorter decision chains, flexible assortments, no in-house private-label teams, and less buying power to impose long payment terms or high listing fees. That last point is a hypothesis to test in interviews.

Risks and regulatory requirements

Regulatory checklist

  • Food (sugar, rice, pulses, pasta): NFSA was established by Law No. 1 of 2017 and sets mandatory labelling rules. Labels must be in Arabic and show product name, manufacturer name and address, brand, origin, ingredients, and production and expiry dates. Packers must hold food-establishment licences. Confirm with NFSA whether each private-label SKU needs its own registration or is covered by the manufacturer's licence. REPORTED (US Commercial Guide, FAO)
  • Factory and trademark registration: GOEIC decrees 992/2015 and 43/2016 cover factory registration for certain imported goods, and include trademark-registration conditions. Register our own trademark (Opportunity A) at the Egyptian Trademark Office before launch.
  • Home and personal care: verify the applicable Egyptian standards and any registration for detergents and hand soap with the manufacturer. Require SDS and batch COA documents. Every performance claim (for example "antibacterial") needs test evidence.
  • Consumer protection and VAT: correct VAT treatment per SKU (sugar exempt; macaroni exempt except semolina; 14% standard on home care) and accurate net weight and volume declarations.

Investment case

Opportunity and proposition

  • Addressable now: modern trade (about 26% of food retail) plus regional chains and wholesalers serving the roughly 60% traditional channel.
  • Customer pain: regional chains have no private-label capability, thin margins on brands, and no QC or sourcing team.
  • Our proposition: a turnkey own-label programme with audited factories, a 25% retailer margin and shelf prices 25–35% below brands.
  • Revenue streams: product margin, programme setup fees, and later our own brand and paid services (design, inspection).
